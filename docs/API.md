# API

Venn's JSON API is served under `/api` by both the Cloudflare Worker and the Node server. Writes must be sent as `application/json`. Times are UTC milliseconds.

- **No key.** Making and answering polls needs no account or key; two tokens returned on creation prove who you are ([Authentication](#authentication)).
- **From your server, not the browser.** The API sends no CORS headers, so a page on another website can't call it with `fetch`. Call it from your server, or show the poll itself with an [embed](EMBED.md).
- **Retries make a second poll.** There's no idempotency key: if a `POST` times out, check before sending it again.

## Make a date poll

`title` and `timezone` (an IANA zone such as `Europe/London`) are required. Then either specific times, each `{ start, end }` in UTC milliseconds on a whole minute:

```sh
curl -X POST https://venn.day/api/events \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"title":"Book club","timezone":"Europe/London","mode":"options","options":[{"start":1894215600000,"end":1894222800000},{"start":1894302000000,"end":1894309200000}]}'
```

or days (`YYYY-MM-DD` in the poll's time zone) and the hours to offer on each, in minutes after midnight, cut into slots of `slotMinutes` (15, 30, 60, or 1440 for whole days):

```sh
curl -X POST https://venn.day/api/events \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"title":"Game night","timezone":"America/New_York","dates":["2030-01-11","2030-01-12"],"startMinute":1080,"endMinute":1320,"slotMinutes":60,"durationMinutes":120}'
```

Whole days are `"startMinute":0,"endMinute":1440,"slotMinutes":1440`, with `durationMinutes` a multiple of 1440 for several days in a row. Each answers `201`:

```json
{
  "id": "cXar7oEEBo",
  "adminToken": "…",
  "url": "https://venn.day/e/cXar7oEEBo",
  "adminUrl": "https://venn.day/e/cXar7oEEBo#admin=…"
}
```

Share `url`. Keep `adminToken` and `adminUrl` private: they manage the poll. `GET /api/events/:id` then lists the poll's `slots`, which are the keys people [answer](#availability) with. To vote on options instead of times, see [Topic polls](#topic-polls).

## Privacy settings

Organizers can switch these on when creating a poll (`POST /api/events`) or later (`PUT /api/events/:id/settings`). The server enforces them, so hidden data never reaches other guests:

| Setting        | What it does                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `requireEmail` | Everyone who answers must send an `email`. Only the organizer sees it: each response has an `email` field for the organizer, and for the person who gave it. |
| `hideNames`    | Other guests see each response named `Guest 1`, `Guest 2`… The answers still show, so everyone can see which dates work.                                     |
| `hideResults`  | Other guests get only their own response, plus `responses`: how many people have answered. The picked date still shows once the organizer picks it.          |

One more on/off setting works the same way: `retro`, the poll's **Retro look**. Its page opens in the retrofuturist look for everyone (`public/retro-app.css`), and its emails come in neon too.

## Deadline and reminders

Any poll, date or vote, can close at a deadline. Send `deadline` (UTC ms, at least an hour ahead and within 30 days) when creating the poll, or later with `PUT /api/events/:id/deadline`. Where the server has sign-in, setting one needs a signed-in organizer (or an app key, `Authorization: Bearer <APP_KEYS entry>`); otherwise `401`. Taking one off (`{ deadline: null }`) never does, and polls that already have one keep it. Everyone sees `deadline` on the poll; from then until a date (or option) is picked, only the organizer can add or change answers (`403` for anyone else).

The organizer can also list `invitees` (up to 50 emails; the poll then asks everyone's email, `requireEmail`, which is how Venn tells who answered, and turning that off drops the invitees but keeps the deadline) and how often to remind them, `remindEvery`: `1`, `2` or `3` days, or `0` for only the day before. When the server sends email, those who haven't answered with that email get a reminder that often, and once more the day before, at most 5 in all; each has a one-click unsubscribe. At the deadline, the organizer gets one email with the best time (or, for a vote, the favourite option) so far: to the email they gave, or else their account's. The invitees' emails are kept with the organizer's private settings and only the organizer sees them; they're deleted with the poll.

The reminders go out from an hourly run: a Cron Trigger on Cloudflare (`wrangler.toml`), and a timer in `server.js`.

## Emailing a poll to people

Send `invitees` (up to 50 emails) when creating a poll, with or without a deadline, and Venn emails each of them the poll straight after: from the organizer's name, with one button to answer (its link fills in their email) and a one-click unsubscribe that also stops reminders. Add or take people off later with `PUT /api/events/:id/invitees`; only people new on the list are emailed, never twice, and not those who already answered. The same list gets the deadline's reminders. Like a deadline, it needs a signed-in organizer where the server has sign-in (`401` otherwise), and nothing is sent when email is off.

To keep it from being used to send mail to strangers, the email holds nothing the organizer writes beyond their name and the poll's title, and there are limits: 100 invitations per poll ever (so taking people off and adding them again doesn't send more) and 200 a day per account, across its polls. Over a limit, the request is refused with `429` and says how many more can go.

## Topic polls

A topic poll decides what or where instead of when: a place, the next book, or one yes-or-no question. People answer each option yes, if needed or no, or pick one when the poll says `single`. Made at `/new/topic` in the app.

```sh
curl -X POST https://venn.day/api/events \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"mode":"topic","title":"Next book","organizer":"Sam","timezone":"Europe/London","options":[{"label":"Piranesi"},{"label":"Dune"}]}'
```

| Field     | What it does                                                                                                                                        |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `mode`    | `"topic"`.                                                                                                                                          |
| `options` | 1 to 20 `{ label }` (or plain strings), up to 120 characters each, no two the same. Each gets an `id` from 1, in order: `options: [{ id, label }]`. |
| `single`  | `true`: everyone picks one option (needs at least two). A yes-or-no question is `options: ["Yes", "No"]` with `single`.                             |

Answers map option ids to `2` (yes) or `1` (if needed), as `availability: { "1": 2, "2": 1 }`; with `single`, one id at most. `slots` is the list of ids. The organizer picks with `POST /api/events/:id/finalize { "option": 1 }`; the poll then has `picked: 1` (and no `finalStart`). Topic polls have no `.ics` file (`404`) and no AI features. To find a date for what was picked, make a date poll with `seriesOf` set to the topic poll's id.

## The next one of a plan

Book clubs, run clubs and game nights come round again. Once the date is picked, the organizer's "Plan the next one" opens `/new/pick?copy=<id>&next` (or `/new/free`): the poll copied, with dates that have passed dropped, or all moved on by whole weeks when none are left, at the same local times whatever daylight saving does. The code is in [`public/copy-poll.js`](../public/copy-poll.js). A plain copy (`?copy=<id>`, "Duplicate poll") does the same without `&next`.

| Field      | What it does                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `seriesOf` | The id of the poll this one follows on from, sent by the app for `&next`. If that poll no longer exists it's left out. Kept when the poll is edited. |

Its organizer can then bring last time's group back from the new poll's page. Polls used to say how often they repeated (`repeat`), with an email to the organizer when it was time; that was taken out, and `repeat` is now ignored.

## Availability

`availability` maps each option's start time (UTC ms) to `2` (yes) or `1` (if needed). Anything missing means no. The keys are the poll's `slots`, exactly as `GET /api/events/:id` lists them: start times for a date poll, and option ids (`1`, `2`…, not the labels) for a [topic poll](#topic-polls).

```json
{ "name": "Ana", "availability": { "1793804400000": 2, "1793818800000": 1 } }
```

A key that isn't one of the poll's `slots` (a time a few milliseconds off, an ISO date, an option's label) isn't saved. The answer is saved without it, and the response says which keys were left out: `{ id, editToken, ignored: ["1793804400005"] }` (the first 20 such keys), or `{ ok: true, ignored: […] }` when changing an answer. When keys were sent and none of them match, the answer is refused with `400`, which is also what a page opened before the organizer changed the poll's times gets: `error` is the message for the person answering ("This poll's times changed after you opened it. Reload the page, then answer again.", or "options" on a topic poll), and `detail` says how to key the answers. An empty `availability` (`{}`, no to everything) is still fine.

## Results

`GET /api/events/:id?results=1` adds `results`, counted on the server the way the poll page and the [MCP server](MCP.md) count them, so you don't have to rank answers yourself. Results hold counts, never names. They show where the answers lean, not what was decided: the organizer's pick is `finalStart` on a date poll and `picked` on a vote, as without `?results=1`, and `leaders` doesn't change when the organizer picks something else.

A [topic poll](#topic-polls) (a vote):

```json
{
  "respondents": 3,
  "options": [
    { "id": 1, "yes": 2, "ifNeeded": 0 },
    { "id": 2, "yes": 2, "ifNeeded": 0 },
    { "id": 3, "yes": 0, "ifNeeded": 1 }
  ],
  "leaders": [1, 2]
}
```

Yes and if needed both count, then more yeses break a tie. `leaders` is every option tied for the lead (here, two), and `[]` until someone says yes or if needed to something.

A date poll gives `slots`, each `{ start, yes, ifNeeded }`; `best`, the 5 best stretches `{ start, end, yes, ifNeeded }`, ranked by most people who can make it, then most yeses, then the earliest (a stretch is the meeting's length, so a trip covers its days in a row); and `leaders`, `[{ start, end }]`. Proposed times and single days can tie, so `leaders` lists every one tied for the top. Hours and runs of days overlap one another, so there `leaders` holds only the first. Times nobody can make aren't in `best`.

When the organizer hides the answers (`hideResults`), only the organizer gets `results`; everyone else gets `responses`, the count, as without it. Asking for results has its own [limit](#too-many-requests-429), 120 per 10 minutes per address, and a plain `GET` doesn't count toward it.

## The poll's picture

An organizer can give a poll any emoji (`emoji` when creating or changing it: one emoji, nothing else) or a picture of their own. The web app makes the picture in the browser: the middle square at 512 × 512, and a 1200 × 630 card with the poll's name for link previews, both JPEG. It uploads them with `PUT /api/events/:id/image` once the poll exists, and again when the poll is edited (so the card has the new name) or copied.

- `icon` is required, at most 200 KB; `card` is optional, at most 300 KB. Each must be a JPEG, PNG or WebP, which the server checks from its first bytes, not from what it says it is.
- `GET /api/events/:id` then includes `image: { v, card, type }`. `v` changes whenever the picture does.
- `icon.jpg` and `card.jpg` serve the picture as itself (`content-type` from its bytes, `x-content-type-options: nosniff`, and a `content-security-policy` of `default-src 'none'; sandbox`). With the current `?v=` they're cached for a year; without it, checked each time.
- With a card, the poll's link preview (`og:image`) is `card.jpg?v=…`; without one, Venn's own. The picked date's `event.ics` lists the icon as an RFC 7986 `IMAGE` (`DISPLAY=BADGE`).
- Deleting the poll deletes its picture.

| `yes`, `maybe`, `no`                              | The answers' own colours (available, if needed, unavailable), `#rrggbb`, each with ink made to read on it. Left out, Venn's green, amber and pink.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `buttons`                                         | `pill` (Venn's own) or `corners`: buttons, pills and switches take the template's corners.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `logoSize`                                        | How tall the logo shows over its polls: `small` (40 px, the default), `medium` (64) or `large` (96).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `hideName`                                        | `true` leaves the name out next to the logo (when the logo already says it); the logo then carries the name for screen readers.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `title`, `note`, `where`                          | What a new poll from it starts with: its name, note for everyone and place.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `emoji`                                           | The poll's emoji, when the organizer doesn't choose one.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `hideVenn`                                        | `true` for [white label](EMBED.md): its polls show no Venn name, logo or link, and their link previews carry the template's name.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `embedSites`                                      | Up to 10 websites (`https://example.com`, just the address) whose pages may show its polls in a frame, at `/embed/<poll>`. See [White label and embedding](EMBED.md).                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

Where sign-in is set up ([Accounts](ACCOUNTS.md)), making a template needs a signed-in session or an app's key (`authorization: Bearer <key>`, one of the server's `APP_KEYS`; see [White label and embedding](EMBED.md#make-templates-from-your-server)); `POST /api/brands` answers 401 without one and the template is kept with that account; without sign-in, no account is needed. Like a poll, a template has two links: the **share link**, `/new?brand=<id>`, for the team (anyone with it can make polls from it, but not change it), and the **manage link**, `/brand/<id>#admin=<adminToken>`, kept private. The app also keeps the token in the browser's local storage.

A poll made with `brand` returns `brand: { id, name, logo, … }` (the fields above that are set) from `GET /api/events/:id`, and its page shows that look (the retro look gives way to it). Editing the poll keeps its brand. Delete the template and its polls go back to Venn's look.

## Authentication

No account is needed. Two secrets, both returned once when they're created, prove who you are:

- **`adminToken`** (from `POST /api/events`): send it as `x-admin-token` for organizer-only calls.
- **`editToken`** (from `POST /api/events/:id/participants`): send it as `x-edit-token` to change or remove that response.

The server stores only SHA-256 hashes of these tokens. In the web app they live in the URL fragment and the browser's local storage.

### Accounts (optional)

When the server has sign-in set up ([Accounts](ACCOUNTS.md)), a signed-in browser carries a session cookie, and polls and templates kept with that account need no `x-admin-token`: organizer calls work with the session (from this site only). `GET /api/events/:id` and `GET /api/brands/:id` give the organizer's view only with `x-organizer: 1`.

| `GET`    | `/api/auth/:provider?next=/me`            | Starts signing in (`google`, `microsoft` or `github`, as listed in `GET /api/config` → `accounts`). Comes back to `next` (a path on this site).                                      |
| `POST`   | `/api/auth/logout`                        | Signs out.                                                                                                                                                                           |

| `DELETE` | `/api/me/polls/:id`, `/api/me/brands/:id` | Takes it off the list; it stays, with its link.                                                                                                                                      |
| `DELETE` | `/api/me`                                 | Deletes the account. Its polls and templates stay. An account with a password sends `{ password }`.                                                                                  |
| `POST`   | `/api/auth/email/signup`                  | `{ email, password }` → `{ ok, email }`, always. Emails a link to `/me/confirm#t=…` (or, for an address with an account, one to choose a new password).                              |

## Errors

Errors come back as JSON with an HTTP status: `{ "error": "This poll does not exist or was deleted" }`. Validation problems are `400`, missing permissions `403`, and unknown polls `404`.

### Too many requests (`429`)

When a visitor (an IP address) sends too much too fast, the request is refused with `429` and the same JSON shape, with a message that says roughly when to try again, for example `{ "error": "You're going a bit fast. Try again in a few minutes." }`. A refused new poll, template or answer also says how long to wait: `Retry-After` is the seconds until one more is allowed, and `RateLimit-Limit`, `RateLimit-Remaining` (`0`) and `RateLimit-Reset` (the same seconds) give the count, so wait that long and retry once. (Where the server uses Cloudflare's rate limiting binding, only `Retry-After: 60` comes back.) The other limits below send no such headers yet, so back off before retrying them. These are the default limits; a server can change them (see [hosting a public instance](MAINTAINING.md#hosting-a-public-instance)):

| What                                                             | Default limit                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| New polls and templates (`POST /api/events`, `POST /api/brands`) | 30 per 10 minutes (`CREATE_RATE_LIMIT`)                                                           |
| Answers (`POST /api/events/:id/participants`)                    | 200 per 10 minutes (`ANSWER_RATE_LIMIT`)                                                          |
| Polls made through [`/api/mcp`](MCP.md)                          | 600 per 10 minutes (`MCP_RATE_LIMIT`), in place of the two above                                  |
| Checking the AI allowance (`GET /api/ai/allowance`)              | 120 per 10 minutes (`AI_ALLOWANCE_RATE_LIMIT`), with `Retry-After` like the three above           |
| [Results](#results) (`GET /api/events/:id?results=1`)            | 120 per 10 minutes (`RESULTS_RATE_LIMIT`), with `Retry-After` like the four above                 |
| AI (`/api/ai/*`, `/api/events/:id/ai/*`)                         | 10 a minute (`AI_RATE_LIMIT`)                                                                     |
| Place search (`GET /api/places`)                                 | 60 a minute (`PLACES_RATE_LIMIT`)                                                                 |
| Messages (`POST /api/contact`)                                   | 3 an hour (`CONTACT_RATE_LIMIT`)                                                                  |
| Email sign-in (`/api/auth/email/*`)                              | 30 per 10 minutes (`AUTH_RATE_LIMIT`), plus a pause after a few wrong passwords or sign-in emails |
| [Emailing a poll](#emailing-a-poll-to-people) (`invitees`)       | 100 per poll ever and 200 a day per account                                                       |

Requests with an app key (`APP_KEYS`) aren't held to the poll, answer and results limits. Each server, and on Cloudflare each Worker instance, counts on its own unless the server has a rate limiting binding, so a limit can be reached a little later than listed. Through MCP, a refused request comes back as a tool error (`isError`) with the same message, not as an HTTP `429`.
